# Master Your Tasks: Prioritization and Time Management

Most men spend their day putting out fires. They wake up, check their phone, answer the loudest email, and spend the next eight hours reacting to other people’s emergencies. By 5 PM, they are exhausted, but when they look back, they realize they accomplished nothing that actually mattered. They were busy, but they were not effective.

The problem is not a lack of time. The problem is a lack of selection.

You cannot do everything. If you try to do everything, you will do nothing well. The difference between a man who is overwhelmed and a man who is in control is simple: the man in control chooses his battles. He identifies the three tasks that move his life forward and executes them with precision. The rest is noise.

This worksheet guides you through the **SELECT** model. It is designed to build your Anvil—the stable surface where the hammer strikes. Without it, you swing randomly. With it, you shape your life. Follow each letter in order. Do not skip steps. Be honest. Be specific.

## S - Sort the Noise

Start by getting everything out of your head. Write down every task, email, errand, and obligation on a single list. This clears your mental RAM. Then, sort them into three buckets: A (Must do today), B (Should do if time allows), and C (Can wait or delegate). This step removes the anxiety of forgetting something and creates a clear hierarchy.

*   **What to avoid:** Listing vague items like "Work on project" or "Clean house." These are too broad to execute.
*   **What works better:** Specific actions like "Draft the introduction for the Q3 report" or "Vacuum the living room."
*   **What to avoid:** Keeping all items in one long, undifferentiated list. This creates decision fatigue.
*   **What works better:** Explicitly labeling each item as A, B, or C. If you can’t label it, you don’t know its priority.

## E - Eliminate the Distractions

Look at your A-list tasks. Identify the top three. These are your "Big Rocks." Now, eliminate the noise around them. Turn off phone notifications, close unrelated browser tabs, and tell others you are in focus mode. You cannot do deep work if you are half-listening to the world. Protect your energy for these three tasks only.

*   **What to avoid:** Keeping your phone on the desk with notifications on while trying to write.
*   **What works better:** Putting the phone in another room or turning on "Do Not Disturb" mode.
*   **What to avoid:** Keeping twenty browser tabs open while working on one specific task.
*   **What works better:** Closing all tabs except the one you are actively using.
*   **What to avoid:** Saying "I’ll just check one email" while working on a deep task.
*   **What works better:** Batching email checks into specific windows outside of your Big Rock blocks.

## L - Lock in the Time

Schedule these three tasks in your calendar as non-negotiable appointments. Put them at the start of your day when your energy is highest. Treat them like a meeting with your boss. If you don’t schedule them, they will get pushed aside by urgent but unimportant tasks. Time blocking turns intention into action.

*   **What to avoid:** Writing tasks on a to-do list but leaving them unscheduled in your day.
*   **What works better:** Putting "Big Rock 1: 8:00 AM - 9:30 AM" directly into your digital or paper calendar.
*   **What to avoid:** Scheduling your hardest task for 4:00 PM when your energy is lowest.
*   **What works better:** Scheduling your hardest task for the first available block after you start work.
*   **What to avoid:** Leaving gaps in your schedule for "maybe" tasks that never get done.
*   **What works better:** Filling the rest of the day with B and C tasks only after the Big Rocks are locked in.

## E - Execute with Focus

Work on the first task until it is done or the time block is up. Use a timer if needed (like the Pomodoro technique: 25 minutes on, 5 minutes off). Do not switch tasks. Switching costs focus. Finish the first A-task completely before starting the second. This builds momentum and gives you a dopamine hit of accomplishment.

*   **What to avoid:** Switching to a different task because the current one feels hard or boring.
*   **What works better:** Staying with the task until the timer rings or the work is complete.
*   **What to avoid:** Multitasking (e.g., listening to a podcast while writing).
*   **What works better:** Single-tasking with full attention on the work at hand.
*   **What to avoid:** Starting the second Big Rock before the first one is finished.
*   **What works better:** Completing the first task, checking it off, and then moving to the second.

## C - Check and Carry Over

At the end of the day, review your list. Check off what you did. If an A-task is unfinished, ask why. Was it too big? Break it down. Was it interrupted? Move it to tomorrow’s top three. Do not add new tasks to tomorrow’s list until you have reviewed today’s. This ensures you are always working on what matters most, not just what is new.

*   **What to avoid:** Adding new, random tasks to tomorrow’s list without reviewing today’s unfinished work.
*   **What works better:** Only adding new tasks if they are critical, and only after moving unfinished A-tasks to tomorrow.
*   **What to avoid:** Ignoring unfinished tasks and hoping they will take care of themselves.
*   **What works better:** Writing down exactly why a task failed (e.g., "Interrupted by client call") to adjust tomorrow’s plan.
*   **What to avoid:** Feeling guilty about unfinished B and C tasks.
*   **What works better:** Accepting that B and C tasks are optional and moving them to tomorrow or deleting them.

## T - Take Back Your Evening

Once the top three are done, you are free. Stop checking email. Stop worrying about the B and C tasks. Enjoy your evening guilt-free. This reward reinforces the habit. You proved you can control your day, so you get to enjoy your night.

*   **What to avoid:** Checking work email at 8:00 PM because you feel you "should" be productive.
*   **What works better:** Closing your laptop and engaging in a hobby or family time.
*   **What to avoid:** Lying in bed worrying about tomorrow’s to-do list.
*   **What works better:** Trusting that your plan is set for tomorrow and resting your mind.
*   **What to avoid:** Doing a "mini-task" (like answering one email) that pulls you back into work mode.
*   **What works better:** Staying completely disconnected until you start your next morning’s routine.

## Closing Push

You have built your Anvil. You have sorted the noise, eliminated distractions, locked in time, and executed with focus. You have checked your work and taken back your evening.

Do this today. Do it tomorrow. Keep doing it until it becomes your nature.

The world will always shout. Emails will ping. Colleagues will ask. Your phone will buzz.

Let them shout.

You have three tasks. You have your Anvil. You have your focus.

Execute.
