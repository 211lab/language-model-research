## Post

There is a coffee mug sitting on the edge of the workbench. It has a chip on the rim. It is stained with the residue of yesterday’s brew. It has been there for three days.

Most men walk past it every morning. They think about washing it. They think about throwing it away. But they don’t. They just keep walking.

This is what happens when we do not select our tasks. We let the small things accumulate. We let the chip on the rim become a pile of dishes. We let the noise of the world drown out the signal of our own life.

The problem is not a lack of time. The problem is a lack of selection.

This week, we are building the Anvil. The structure that holds your focus. The stable surface where the hammer strikes. Without it, you are just swinging in the air, reacting to every ping and buzz until you are exhausted but empty.

To build the Anvil, you need a plan. Not a vague intention, but a concrete structure.

That is why the worksheet is ready.

This is not just a list. It is the execution tool for the week’s lesson. It forces you to sort the noise, eliminate the distractions, lock in the time, and execute with focus. It is the practical artifact we will be working from.

The lecture and the essay will give you the why. The worksheet gives you the how.

Do not wait until the discussion to start. The conversation will be richer if you have already done the work. If you have already felt the weight of the hammer and the stability of the Anvil.

Open the worksheet. Sort your tasks. Select your top three.

Let’s see what you build.
