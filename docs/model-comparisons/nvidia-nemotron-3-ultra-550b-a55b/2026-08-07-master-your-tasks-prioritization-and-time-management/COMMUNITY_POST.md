## Post

The coffee sits cooling beside the keyboard. Forty-seven messages wait in the inbox. Slack pings three times before a single document opens. By noon the badges are cleared, two meetings attended, every question answered — yet the report that would actually move the project forward sits untouched. The day feels busy. The body feels tired. But nothing finished matters.

This isn't a discipline problem. The discipline exists — it's spent answering emails, attending meetings, clearing badges. The problem is structural. There's no mechanism to convert the insight of what matters into the action of doing it. Pressure without a path becomes heat without combustion.

This week we're building that mechanism.

The worksheet walks through the PRIORITIZE model — nine steps that form a channel from morning clarity to evening completion. Purge everything onto paper so mental RAM clears. Rank by impact so the A-tasks declare themselves. Identify the prime energy window and organize the schedule around it. Remove friction before starting. Iterate in sprints. Track completion. Inspect the day's data. Zero in on tomorrow's three before sleep. Expand what works weekly.

Ten minutes each morning. Ninety minutes of guarded focus. A visible scorecard at shutdown.

The lecture and lesson break down each step with examples and troubleshooting. But the worksheet is where the metal meets the mold. It's the practical artifact we'll all be working from — the same page, same structure, same daily cadence.

Do the worksheet before the group conversation. Come with your first week's scorecards, your friction points, the adjustment you wrote for tomorrow. The discussion isn't theoretical — it's a comparison of pours. What held. What spilled. What the trough needs next week.

The cast house run does not run itself. It must be run.
