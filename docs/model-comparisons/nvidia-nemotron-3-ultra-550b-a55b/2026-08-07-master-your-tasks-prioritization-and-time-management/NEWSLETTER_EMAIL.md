## Subject
Stop Reacting, Start Leading Your Day — This Week's System

## Body
The coffee sits cooling beside your keyboard. Forty-seven messages wait. Slack pings before you open a single document. By noon the badges are cleared, two meetings attended, every question answered — yet the report that would actually move your project forward sits untouched. You feel busy. You feel tired. You cannot name one thing you finished that matters.

This is the trap: reactivity masquerading as productivity. The loudest demands claim your best hours. The important work — the work that compounds, the work that builds leverage — gets pushed to "later," which becomes tomorrow, which becomes next week. Friday arrives and Monday's big goal never got touched. The guilt is vague but persistent, carried from day to day like a stone in the pocket.

The problem isn't discipline. You have discipline — it's spent answering emails, attending meetings, clearing badges. The problem is structural. There's no mechanism to convert the insight of what matters into the action of doing it. Pressure without a path becomes heat without combustion. The realization of your priorities arrives. The urgency is felt. The intention forms. But intention is not a pour. The metal cools in the ladle. The mold stays empty.

This week's worksheet is that mechanism. The PRIORITIZE model — Purge, Rank, Identify, Organize, Remove, Iterate, Track, Inspect, Zero in, Expand — is a refractory-lined channel that carries molten insight from "these three things matter" to "I am doing the first one right now." Ten minutes each morning. Ninety minutes of guarded focus. A visible scorecard at shutdown. Each day starts sharper than the last.

Your task: open the attached worksheet and complete every section before tomorrow morning. Write tomorrow's three A-tasks on paper tonight — specific, not vague. Tomorrow, before email, before chat, before any input, purge your head onto that same paper. Rank. Identify your 90-minute prime window. Block it: CAST HOUSE RUN — DO NOT MOVE. Remove friction: phone in drawer, tabs closed, water ready, sign up. Start the first sprint the moment the block begins. Work only the first A-task until complete or the block ends. Check it off visibly. Note what pulled you off course. At shutdown, inspect: did the top three get done? What derailed? What made focus easy? Write one adjustment. Zero in on the next morning's three. Do this for five consecutive weekdays. No exceptions.
