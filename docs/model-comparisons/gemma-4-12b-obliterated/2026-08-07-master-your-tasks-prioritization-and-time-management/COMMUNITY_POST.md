## Post

When a builder knows what needs to be built, they follow a blueprint that dictates the order of operations. They know that the foundation must be poured before the roof can be installed. If a builder were to choose to install a window simply because the glass was available or the request was immediate, they would be ignoring the blueprint in favor of the moment.

In many daily workflows, however, the construction project is replaced by a constant exercise in firefighting. When a task is urgent, it demands attention. When a task is important, it requires a choice. If no choice is made, the urgent will always win. This leaves the most critical projects unfinished and the most important goals out of reach.

To move from this reactive posture to a proactive one, we must establish a clear hierarchy. We are moving toward a model where we identify the "Big Rocks"—the high-impact tasks that drive the most progress—and ensure they are completed before minor errands and distractions can consume the available time.

This week, we are tackling this transition by building a structured path to eliminate chaos. We will be exploring how to move from feeling overwhelmed by a massive list of tasks to having a clear roadmap for the day.

To prepare for our upcoming group conversation, you will be working through a specific worksheet designed to help you identify and prioritize these high-impact tasks. This worksheet serves as your primary tool for building the hierarchy required to move from a disorganized to-do list to a controlled, efficient workflow.

Completing this worksheet is a critical step in this transformation. It allows you to move from the theory of prioritization to the practice of identifying the "Big Rocks" and protecting the time required to complete them. By engaging with this worksheet now, you will be prepared to discuss the practical challenges of isolating and executing these tasks during our group session.

Get ready to stop reacting to requests and start enforcing a hierarchy of work.
