## Model
**F - Find All Tasks:** Start by doing a "brain dump." Write down every single thing you need to do—big projects, small errands, work duties, personal chores. Get it all out of your head and onto paper or into a digital list. This clears your mind and gives you a complete picture of your workload.
**O - Organize by Importance:** Now, sort your list. Ask yourself: "Is this task important, or is it just urgent?" Use a system (like the Eisenhower Matrix) to label tasks. A-level tasks are the most important and impactful. B-level tasks are important but less urgent. C-level tasks are minor chores. Also, identify tasks you can delegate or postpone.
**C - Commit to the Top Tasks:** Select your top 2 or 3 A-level tasks—your "big rocks"—for the day. Block out specific time in your schedule to work on these tasks first, when your energy is highest. Treat these time blocks like non-negotiable appointments.
**U - Uninterruptible Deep Work:** When you start a priority task, eliminate all distractions. Turn off your phone notifications, close unnecessary tabs, and tell people you are in focus mode. Use techniques like the Pomodoro method (short bursts of intense work followed by short breaks) to keep your focus sharp and maintain momentum.
**S - Systematically Review and Adjust:** At the end of the day, review your list. Check off what you finished. Carry over anything unfinished to tomorrow, making sure to reprioritize it. Ask yourself: Did I focus on the right things? Where did I get distracted? Use this review to improve your plan for the next day.

## Action Steps Summary
**1. Brain Dump and Sort:** Capture every task and immediately prioritize them into A, B, and C levels to know what truly matters.
**2. Schedule Your Big Rocks:** Block out prime time in your calendar specifically for your top 2-3 most important tasks each day.
**3. Focus and Review:** Eliminate distractions during work sprints and use a daily review to ensure your plan is working and adjust for tomorrow.

## FAQ
**Q: I have too many tasks to even start. What should I do?**
A: Start with a "brain dump." Just write everything down first. Don't try to organize it yet; just get it out of your head so you can see the full scope.
**Q: How do I know if a task is "important" or just "urgent"?**
A: Ask yourself: "Does completing this task move me closer to my biggest goals?" If the answer is no, it's likely urgent but not important.
**Q: What if I can't block out time for my big tasks?**
A: Look for small pockets of time. Even 25 minutes of focused work on a big task is better than hours of distracted effort.
**Q: How do I stop myself from getting distracted by my phone?**
A: When you start a priority task, put your phone on silent and out of sight. Treat your focus time like a meeting you cannot miss.
**Q: Is it okay to delegate tasks?**
A: Yes, it is a key part of prioritization. If a task is important but someone else can do it, delegate it to free up your time for your "big rocks."
