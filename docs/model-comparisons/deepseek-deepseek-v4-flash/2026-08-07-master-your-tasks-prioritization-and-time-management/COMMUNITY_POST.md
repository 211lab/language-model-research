## Post

You wake up with good intentions. By noon, you’ve answered fifteen emails, put out two fires for other people, and the one project that would actually move your career forward hasn’t been touched. You end the day exhausted, wondering where the time went, feeling guilty that you’re busy but not effective.

That’s not a time problem. That’s a prioritization problem disguised as a busy schedule. And the cost isn’t just lost productivity—it’s the slow erosion of your sense of control. You start believing you just can’t finish important things. That belief becomes a habit. That habit becomes your identity.

This week, we’re cutting that cycle. Not with more discipline or another motivational speech. With a system that forces the right tasks to the top and keeps the noise out. A system that turns your chaotic to-do list into a clear, executable plan.

The lesson and lecture will walk you through the mechanics—how to filter, order, protect, and execute. But the real work happens in the worksheet. That’s the artifact we’ll be working from. It’s the tool that turns the theory into a daily practice. You’ll use it to brain dump, pick your top three impact tasks, block protected time, and review your progress. It’s designed to be your blower—the mechanism that regulates your attention so you stop reacting and start directing.

Here’s the urgency: you have seven days. Starting tomorrow morning, you’ll implement this system. The worksheet is your guide. Without it, the lecture is just information. With it, you have a concrete move to make before we gather to discuss what worked, what didn’t, and where you got stuck.

Do the worksheet before the conversation. Bring your results—the wins, the slips, the questions. That’s where the real transformation happens: not in the theory, but in the doing, then in the sharing.

The fire is waiting. The blower is in your hands. Let’s get to work.
