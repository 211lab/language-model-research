# Instructions: Master Your Tasks

Most people do not have a productivity problem; they have a prioritization problem. You likely spend your days in a state of frantic motion—answering every ping and responding to every "quick favor"—only to realize at 6:00 PM that you haven't touched the one project that actually matters. This is the "whack-a-mole" existence. You are currently a slave to the loudest voice in the room because your brain is wired to respond to urgency rather than importance.

To stop reacting and start controlling, you must stop treating your to-do list as a suggestion and start treating it as a map. The goal of this exercise is to implement the FILTER model to separate the noise from the needle-movers, eliminating the "midnight panic" and ensuring your energy is directed toward your highest-leverage outcomes.

## F - Flush the System
Your mental RAM is currently cluttered with open loops, worries, and errands. You cannot prioritize what you cannot see. In this section of the worksheet, perform a total brain dump. Write down every single task, errand, and worry currently occupying your mind, regardless of how small or insignificant it seems.

*   **Avoid:** Writing only the "important" things or trying to organize the list as you go.
*   **Works Better:** A raw, unfiltered list of everything from "Finish Q4 Strategy" to "Buy lightbulbs."

## I - Identify the Rocks
Now that everything is visible, you must apply the Eisenhower Matrix to separate the Urgent from the Important. Review your brain dump and identify your "Big Rocks"—the 2-3 tasks that provide the highest leverage. These are the tasks that, if completed, make the day an absolute win regardless of what else happens.

*   **Avoid:** Listing 10 "top priorities" or picking tasks that are merely urgent (like clearing an inbox) but not important.
*   **Works Better:** Selecting 3 specific, high-impact tasks that move you closer to a long-term goal.

## L - Lock the Calendar
A to-do list is a wish list of burdens; a schedule is a plan for execution. Assign your Big Rocks to specific time blocks on your calendar. Place these during your peak energy hours. These are no longer "tasks to get to"—they are non-negotiable appointments with yourself.

*   **Avoid:** Writing "Work on Project X" without a start and end time.
*   **Works Better:** Blocking "9:00 AM – 11:00 AM: Draft Project X Proposal."

## T - Target the Focus
Execution fails when you succumb to the "switching cost" of jumping between tasks. For each of your locked time blocks, define your focus protocol. Use the Pomodoro technique or deep-work sprints. Explicitly list the distractions you will eliminate to ensure one task gets 100% of your attention.

*   **Avoid:** Vague intentions like "I'll try to stay focused."
*   **Works Better:** "Phone in other room, Slack closed, 25-minute timer set."

## E - Execute the Batch
Low-value tasks (B and C level) are energy leaks. If you answer emails as they arrive, you are letting others dictate your day. In this section, group all similar low-energy tasks—emails, chores, quick calls—into one single batch block later in the day.

*   **Avoid:** Scattering small tasks throughout your high-energy morning blocks.
*   **Works Better:** "4:00 PM – 5:00 PM: Batch all emails, returns calls, and admin."

## R - Review and Reset
The system only works if it is maintained. Define your shutdown ritual. You will check off your wins to build momentum and evaluate the tasks that didn't get done. Most importantly, you must set your top 3 Big Rocks for tomorrow before you finish your workday today.

*   **Avoid:** Simply closing your laptop and hoping you remember what to do tomorrow.
*   **Works Better:** A written list of tomorrow's 3 Big Rocks and a checked-off list of today's victories.

***

Complete every section of the worksheet with absolute honesty. If you are vague, your plan will fail. Be specific, be disciplined, and stop apologizing for ignoring the noise. Fill out the worksheet now and execute your Big Rocks before 2:00 PM tomorrow.
