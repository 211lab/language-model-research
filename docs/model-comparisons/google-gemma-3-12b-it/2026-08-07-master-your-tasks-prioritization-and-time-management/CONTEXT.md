## Model
**S**ort: Begin by creating a comprehensive list of all tasks, both big and small, personal and professional. Don't filter or judge; just get everything out of your head and onto paper (or a digital tool).
**T**riage: Categorize each task using the Eisenhower Matrix (Urgent/Important). Be honest with yourself about what's truly important.
**R**eschedule: Schedule your "Important" tasks into your calendar, treating them like non-negotiable appointments. Break down large tasks into smaller, manageable steps.
**U**nload: Delegate tasks whenever possible. If something can be done by someone else, let it go.
**C**omplete: Focus on completing one task at a time, eliminating distractions and working with focused intensity. Use techniques like the Pomodoro method to maintain concentration.
**T**rack: Regularly review your progress, adjusting your priorities and schedule as needed. Celebrate your accomplishments, no matter how small.
**U**nderstand: Reflect on your task management habits. What's working well? What needs improvement? Continuously refine your system to optimize your productivity.

## Action Steps Summary
*   **Brainstorm Blitz:** Quickly list *everything* you need to do, clearing your mind. (Outcome: A complete picture of your workload.)
*   **Prioritize Power:** Categorize tasks by importance and urgency, focusing on the vital few. (Outcome: A clear hierarchy of what matters most.)
*   **Schedule Success:** Block time for your top priorities, protecting your focus and ensuring progress. (Outcome: A roadmap for achieving your goals.)

## FAQ
*   **Q: I’m already busy – how can I fit in another system?** A: This isn't about adding *more* to your plate, but about working *smarter* on what matters most. It’s about eliminating the unnecessary.
*   **Q: What if a task is both urgent and important?** A: Schedule it immediately and protect that time fiercely.
*   **Q: I keep getting interrupted. What can I do?** A: Communicate your need for focused time to colleagues and family. Turn off notifications and create a dedicated workspace.
*   **Q: How do I know if a task is *really* important?** A: Does it directly contribute to your goals or values? If not, consider delegating or eliminating it.
*   **Q: I’m afraid of saying “no.”** A: Saying “no” to unimportant requests frees you up to say “yes” to what truly matters. It’s a form of self-respect.
