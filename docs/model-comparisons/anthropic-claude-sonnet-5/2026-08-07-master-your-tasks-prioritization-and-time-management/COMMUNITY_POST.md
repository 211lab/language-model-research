## Post

There's an anvil in my neighbor's garage that hasn't moved in forty years. Black, pitted, worn into a swayback dip from decades of strikes. He told me once that the anvil never does the work. The hammer does that. The anvil just holds things still long enough for the hit to count.

I think about that more than I should, mostly because I know what it feels like to swing all day and shape nothing.

Forty emails. Three fires put out. A meeting that could've been a text. Six o'clock rolls around and you're tired in that specific way, the kind that comes from constant motion, but you'd struggle to name one thing that actually moved forward. Meanwhile the task that mattered got pushed again. Tomorrow. Then the day after. Until it stopped being a task and became an emergency.

That's not a discipline problem. It's a sorting problem. Urgent and important feel identical at 7 AM, but they are not the same size, and if you can't tell them apart before the noise starts, the noise decides for you.

This week is about building the thing that lets you tell them apart. Not a mindset. Not a mood. A fixed place to sort from, so effort finally has something to press against instead of just burning hot and going nowhere.

That's what the worksheet is this week. It's not extra homework sitting next to the lesson — it's the surface you'll actually work on. The lecture and lesson exist to make sure you know how to use it right: how to target everything crowding your head, rank it by real impact instead of volume, isolate what doesn't deserve today, and guard the hours you give to what's left.

Skip the worksheet and the lesson stays theory. Do it, and you show up to group with something real — a list that actually got sorted, a block that actually got guarded, a day that actually got evaluated instead of just survived.

We're going to talk through what happened when you tried it. Where it held. Where it slipped. Where the metal moved and where it just rolled off the table again.

Pick your three before that conversation. Prove it by sundown.
