## Actionable VERB
FOCUS

## Model
F – Find your real tasks
- Write down every task on your mind in one place (notes app, notebook, or planner).
- Include work, home, and personal tasks.
- Don’t organize yet—just capture everything so nothing is hidden in your head.

O – Order by impact
- For each task, ask: “If I only finish 3 things today, which ones would make the biggest difference?”
- Mark those as A (top priority).
- Mark next-level tasks as B (important but not urgent).
- Mark small or flexible tasks as C.
- Cross out or move tasks that don’t truly matter.

C – Choose your big rocks
- Each day, pick 2–3 A tasks that align with your main goals or responsibilities.
- These are your “big rocks.”
- Everything else is secondary.
- If a task doesn’t support your key goals or duties, it’s not a big rock.

U – Use time blocks
- Put your big rocks into your calendar as real appointments.
- Schedule them when your energy is highest (often morning).
- Group similar small tasks (emails, calls, admin) into one block.
- Treat these blocks as non-negotiable.

S – Shield your attention
- During a priority block:
  - Silence notifications.
  - Close unrelated tabs.
  - Tell others you’re in focus mode.
- Use short sprints (for example: 25 minutes work, 5 minutes break).
- Stay on one task until it’s done or the block ends.

## Action Steps Summary
1. Capture and classify
   - Write all tasks down.
   - Label them A, B, or C by impact.
   - Benefit: Clear list, no mental clutter.

2. Pick and schedule your big rocks
   - Choose 2–3 A tasks daily.
   - Block time for them first.
   - Benefit: Guaranteed progress on what matters.

3. Protect and execute
   - Remove distractions during priority blocks.
   - Use short focused sprints.
   - Benefit: Faster completion, less stress, more done.

## FAQ
- “I have too many tasks to prioritize.”
  - Start by listing them all, then mark only the 3 that would make the biggest difference today. Everything else is not top priority.

- “My boss/clients keep sending urgent requests.”
  - Use an urgent vs important check: if it’s truly urgent and important, add it as an A task and move something else. If it’s just loud, schedule it in a lower-priority block.

- “I get distracted easily.”
  - Use 25-minute focus sprints with a timer. Close extra tabs, silence phone, and work on one task only until the timer ends.

- “I feel guilty saying no.”
  - You can’t do everything. Saying no or delegating protects your ability to do your best work on what truly matters.

- “What if I don’t finish everything?”
  - That’s normal. At the end of the day, move unfinished A tasks to tomorrow’s list and reprioritize. Cut or shrink tasks that never get done.
