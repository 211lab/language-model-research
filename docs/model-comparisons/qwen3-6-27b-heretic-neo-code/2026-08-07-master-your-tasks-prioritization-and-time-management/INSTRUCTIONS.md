You are not busy. You are scattered.

Right now, your day is controlled by whoever shouts the loudest. You react to noise instead of leading your work. You treat every task like it’s equally important, so nothing important gets done. You fill your schedule with motion, not progress.

This worksheet is not a generic planner. It is a tool to help you:

- Decide each day what truly counts.
- Put that work first.
- Protect it like your job depends on it.

Because it does.

You will move through the FOCUS model, one letter at a time. Each section tells you exactly what to write in the corresponding part of the worksheet. Be specific. Be honest. If your answers are vague, your day will be vague.

Use this worksheet daily for the next 7 days.

F – Find your real tasks

In the “F – Find your real tasks” section of the worksheet, do a complete brain dump.

- Write every task currently on your mind in one place:
  - Work tasks
  - Home tasks
  - Personal tasks
- Include:
  - Projects
  - Emails to send
  - Calls to make
  - Meetings to prepare for
  - Errands
  - Anything you feel guilty about not doing
- Do not organize yet. Do not judge. Just get it out of your head.

What to avoid:
- Leaving tasks “in your head” because they feel obvious.
- Writing only “work stuff” and ignoring personal responsibilities.
- Vague entries like “stuff” or “admin” without specifying what that means.

What works better:
- Concrete items: “Draft Q3 report outline,” “Call dentist,” “Reply to client X about pricing.”
- One unified list: all tasks in the same place, not scattered across notes and tabs.
- Enough detail that another person could understand what you need to do.

O – Order by impact

In the “O – Order by impact” section, prioritize your list by impact, not noise.

For each task, ask:
- “If I only finish 3 things today, which ones would make the biggest difference?”

Then label each task:
- A: Top priority. Directly moves a key goal or responsibility.
- B: Important, but not urgent or second-tier.
- C: Small, flexible, or low-impact.
- Cross out or move tasks that don’t truly matter.

What to avoid:
- Marking everything as A.
- Letting “urgent” override “important” without checking.
- Keeping tasks you know you won’t do and don’t need to do.

What works better:
- Limiting A tasks to the 3–5 that would genuinely move you forward today.
- Using B for tasks that matter but can wait or be batched.
- Using C for quick or low-impact tasks you can do in spare time.
- Removing or postponing tasks that don’t align with your key goals or responsibilities.

C – Choose your big rocks

In the “C – Choose your big rocks” section, select your 2–3 A tasks that align with your main goals or responsibilities.

These are your big rocks. Everything else is secondary.

- Write each big rock as a clear, specific task.
- Ensure each one:
  - Directly supports a key goal or duty.
  - Is something you can realistically work on today.

What to avoid:
- Choosing big rocks that are too vague: “Work on project” instead of “Complete slide deck for Monday’s presentation.”
- Picking tasks that feel easy but don’t move you forward.
- Selecting more than 3 big rocks and diluting your focus.

What works better:
- Big rocks that, if completed, would make today feel like a success.
- Tasks that reduce future stress or move long-term goals forward.
- A short, focused list: 2–3 items you are committed to finishing.

U – Use time blocks

In the “U – Use time blocks” section, schedule your big rocks and related work as real appointments.

- Put each big rock into your calendar or schedule:
  - Choose specific time slots.
  - Prefer times when your energy is highest (often morning).
- Group similar small tasks (emails, calls, admin) into one block.
- Treat these blocks as non-negotiable.

What to avoid:
- Leaving big rocks “unscheduled” and hoping you’ll get to them.
- Scheduling big rocks only at the end of the day when you’re tired.
- Overloading your schedule with blocks that don’t match realistic capacity.

What works better:
- Clear blocks: “10:00–11:00: Draft proposal for Client X.”
- A dedicated block for B and C tasks, not scattered all day.
- A schedule that protects time for your most important work before noise takes over.

S – Shield your attention

In the “S – Shield your attention” section, define how you will protect your focus during priority blocks.

For each priority block, specify:
- What you will silence (phone, notifications, email).
- What you will close (unrelated tabs, apps, documents).
- How you will signal to others you’re in focus mode (status, message, door closed, etc.).
- How you will structure your work (for example: 25 minutes work, 5 minutes break).

What to avoid:
- Leaving your phone on and visible.
- Keeping email or messaging apps open while working on big rocks.
- Allowing interruptions without a clear boundary or signal.

What works better:
- A simple, repeatable routine: “During priority blocks, I silence notifications, close unrelated tabs, and work in 25-minute sprints.”
- A visible cue: “Do not disturb” status or a short message to colleagues.
- Staying on one task until it’s done or the block ends.

Closing

This worksheet is only useful if you act on what you write.

Each day:
- Capture and classify your tasks.
- Pick 2–3 big rocks.
- Schedule them.
- Protect your focus.
- Review what you completed and adjust tomorrow.

No day without big rocks.
No big rocks without scheduled time.
No scheduled time without protected focus.

If you do this for 7 days, you will:
- Complete more of your most important tasks.
- Reduce time on low-value busywork.
- Finish your workday earlier, without dropping key responsibilities.

If you don’t, you keep the same scattered routine.

Decide now. Fill out this worksheet. Then lead your day.
