## Post

There is a coffee mug sitting on the edge of a workbench. It’s chipped, stained, and hasn’t moved all morning.

Around it, the day is already falling apart.

A message pops up. A coworker asks for “a quick thing.” A client sends an “urgent” note. You answer. You switch tabs. You check email. You feel productive.

By evening, you are exhausted, behind, and unsure what you actually accomplished.

The mug is still there.

You didn’t move it. You didn’t need to. It’s just sitting where you left it, while everything else around it got noisy.

That’s the shape of your day.

You are not busy. You are scattered.

Right now, your day is controlled by whoever shouts the loudest. You react to noise instead of leading your work. You treat every task like it’s equally important, so nothing important gets done. You fill your schedule with motion, not progress.

If you do not choose what matters, everything will pretend to matter—and you will lose.

This week, we’re going to change that.

We’re focusing on one thing: how to prioritize your tasks and protect your time so you actually get the important work done—without chaos.

To make this real, you’ll be using a worksheet that becomes your weekly command center. It’s not a theory exercise. It’s the practical artifact we’ll all be working from.

Here’s how it fits:

- The lesson and lecture will show you how to use the worksheet clearly and consistently.
- The worksheet will help you:
  - Capture all your tasks in one place.
  - Sort them by impact, not noise.
  - Choose your “big rocks” each day.
  - Block time for them and protect your focus.
- The group conversation will be about what’s actually happening when you run this system—where it’s working, where it’s breaking, and how to adjust.

So here’s the ask:

- Complete the worksheet before our discussion.
- Don’t skim it. Use it on your real tasks.
- Come ready to talk about what you noticed, what felt hard, and what you’re going to keep doing.

We’re not here to collect busywork. We’re here to build a simple system that helps you:
- Get ahead at work without working later.
- Make steady progress on long-term goals.
- Reduce last-minute stress.
- Protect time for family, health, and rest.

If you do this week’s worksheet honestly, you’ll start to feel the difference: fewer scattered hours, more real progress, less guilt.

If you don’t, you keep the same routine. Same noise. Same exhaustion.

Your move.

FOCUS.
