## Post

There is a coffee mug sitting on the corner of a workbench. It is chipped on the rim. There is a ring of dried coffee at the bottom. It has been there for three days. The person who used it did not forget it. They were just too busy moving to stop and clean it up. They were running from one task to another, answering emails, fixing small problems, and putting out fires. They felt productive. They felt necessary. But when they looked at the bench, they saw only clutter.

The mug was not the problem. The lack of direction was.

We mistake motion for progress. We think that if we are tired, we must have done something important. But exhaustion is not a measure of value. It is often just a measure of friction. When you are scattered, your day is dictated by the loudest voice in the room. An email pings, so you answer it. A small, easy task appears, so you do it to feel a quick hit of accomplishment. You are reacting to noise instead of creating signal.

The result is a quiet anxiety. It is the feeling of knowing you have a mountain of work waiting for tomorrow. It is the guilt of leaving your desk while your big projects sit untouched. You feel like you ran a marathon, but when you look at the map, you are still standing at the starting line.

This is not a problem of time. You have the same twenty-four hours as everyone else. This is a problem of structure.

To fix this, we need a mechanical process. We need a way to control the intake of air. We need a way to direct our attention. This week, we are installing that structure.

Your worksheet for this week is the practical artifact for this shift. It is not a summary of the lecture. It is the tool you will use to filter your list, order your day, and cut distractions. The lesson and lecture provide the support for using this worksheet well, but the worksheet is where the work happens.

Do not wait for the group discussion to start thinking about this. The discussion is where we troubleshoot the friction you encounter. If you do not do the worksheet, you will have nothing to bring to the table. You will be talking about theory instead of reality.

Use the next few days to run the FOCUS model on your actual tasks. Filter your list. Order your day. Cut distractions. Use your energy wisely. Stop and review.

Bring your results, your failures, and your questions to the group. We will discuss what worked, what broke, and how to tighten the system.

The coffee mug is still there. It is still dirty. But now you see it differently. It is a symbol of a day that was not finished. It is a reminder that motion is not progress.

You can leave it there. You can keep running. Or you can pick it up. You can wash it. You can put it away. You can clear the bench. You can start fresh.

Your fire is waiting. Feed it.
