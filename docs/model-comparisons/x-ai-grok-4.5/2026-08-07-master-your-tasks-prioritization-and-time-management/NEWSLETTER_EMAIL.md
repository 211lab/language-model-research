## Subject
Rank Three. Block Them First. Finish What Matters.

## Body
You already know the feeling: a full day, a tired body, and the three tasks that would have moved real work still sitting unfinished.

This week is about ending that pattern. Busy is not finished. A long list is not a plan. When chat, favors, errands, and mixed chores vote first, the day runs you—and the same priorities roll into tomorrow while evenings fill with work that should have been closed before noon. Order is the problem, not effort. If you do not choose the top three before the day chooses for you, loud work wins and high-impact work waits.

This week’s public letter, *Finish What Matters Before the Day Runs You*, names that leak clearly: pressure without a path scatters into reaction and half-work. It shows why awareness and longer lists are not enough, and why the fix is a hard morning filter—dump everything, isolate three outcome-changing tasks, label the rest so it stops competing, put the top three in prime hours, guard those blocks, then check off and reset. The promise is practical and measurable: within seven days, complete your top three on at least five workdays before noon, with a written A/B/C list each morning and fewer than two unplanned switches during each priority block. Read the letter. Let it set the standard before you touch the page.

Do the work on the worksheet.

Here is what you are expected to do with it. Work FILTER in order, with specific honest answers—not vague categories. **F:** dump every task on your mind for the day (work, home, errands, follow-ups) one action per line. **I:** circle exactly three A-tasks—the ones that create the biggest positive result if they are all you finish. **L:** mark the rest B or C, and note what you will delay, drop, or hand off. **T:** place each A-task in a real start–end block in your best energy hours, before noon when you can, and batch B/C later. **E:** write how you will silence noise, run short sprints, and stay under two unplanned switches. **R:** at day’s end, check off what finished, move what remains, re-rank tomorrow, and cut or split anything that has rolled for days. Fill it as if the day will test every weak line. Repeat for seven days and track the standard in writing.
