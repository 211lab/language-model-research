## Post

The yellow legal pad sat open on the kitchen table at 9:40 p.m. Three lines near the top were still blank where the real work should have been checked off. Below them, a mess of smaller marks: answered texts, a returned call, two errands, a favor, a quick fix that ate forty minutes. The coffee had gone cold hours ago. The day was full. The body was tired. The work that mattered had not moved.

Most of us know that pad. Not always paper. Sometimes a notes app. Sometimes a half-remembered list in the head. You never stopped moving, and somehow you still finished behind.

Busy is not finished. A long list is not a plan. When chat pings open the morning, meetings land on your sharpest hours, and chores sit in the same blur as the hard projects, the loud work votes first. The big rocks stay on the floor. You carry the same priority across the week. Evenings fill with must-do work that should have been closed before noon.

This week is about stopping that pattern—picking the right three, doing them first, and leaving the noise for later. Not by working harder. By restoring order before the day chooses for you.

The worksheet is the concrete move. It is the artifact you will fill, rank, block, and guard from. Lesson and lecture exist to help you use it well—so the dump is honest, the top three are real, the blocks hold, and the end-of-day reset actually closes the loop. Do the worksheet before we meet. Bring what you wrote, what slipped, and what you protected. That is the conversation: not theory about prioritization, but the page in front of you and whether the pour happened before the first ping.

You already know how the week goes if nothing changes. Rank three. Block them first. Guard the run. Or keep reacting and own the pile. Fill the worksheet now so we have something real to work from when we talk.
