## Title
Finish What Matters Before the Day Runs You

## Subtitle
Stop drowning in busywork. Learn a simple way to pick the right tasks, do them first, and leave the noise for later.

## Promise
Within 7 days, complete your top 3 priority tasks on at least 5 workdays before noon, with a written A/B/C list each morning and fewer than 2 unplanned task switches during each priority block.

## Motivations
- Get the work that moves goals forward done early, not late
- Keep projects from piling up across the week
- Free evenings from leftover “must-do” tasks
- Show up prepared for meetings with key items already finished
- Build a daily record of finished high-impact work you can point to

## Challenges & Sacrifices
- A full inbox and chat stream that demand replies before real work starts
- Meetings stacked over the hours when focus is strongest
- A long mixed list of errands, chores, and deep work with no clear order
- Shared family or team needs that pull time without warning
- Tools and tabs open all day that break every work sprint

## Frustrations
- Ending the day tired after doing a lot, yet missing the three things that counted
- Watching small urgent requests push big tasks to tomorrow again
- Starting deep work, then losing the block to messages and “quick” favors
- Carrying the same unfinished priority item across many days
- Feeling behind even when the calendar was full

## Transformations
- From finishing only what shouted loudest → to finishing the three tasks that change results
- From a messy catch-all list → to a ranked A/B/C list you follow in order
- From scattered half-work → to protected focus blocks that close real items
- From endless carryover → to clear done checks and a short next-day list
- From reacting all day → to running a simple plan you can repeat

## Choice
You can keep living the hard way: react to every ping, stay busy, and watch the real work slip. Or you can take action now, rank what matters, and start the path to finishing your top three before the day takes over.

## Model
**F — Fill the full list**  
Write down every task on your mind for the day: work, home, errands, follow-ups. Get it out of your head and onto one page or one app so nothing hides.

**I — Isolate the big rocks**  
Ask: if I only finish three things today, which three create the biggest positive result? Circle those as your A-tasks. These are your big rocks.

**L — Label the rest A/B/C**  
Mark A for the top three. Mark B for important work that can wait until A is moving. Mark C for small chores. Note what you will delay, drop, or hand off. You will not do everything today.

**T — Time-block the A-tasks first**  
Put A-tasks in your best energy hours, early if you can. Give each a real block on your schedule. Batch B and C work later (calls together, errands together). Treat A-blocks like fixed appointments.

**E — Eliminate noise and execute**  
When an A-block starts, silence the phone, close extra tabs, and tell others you are in focus mode. Use short sprints (such as 25 minutes on, 5 minutes off). Work the A-task until it is done or the block ends.

**R — Review, check off, and reset**  
At day’s end, check off finished work. Move what is left to tomorrow and rank it again. If the same item never gets done, cut it or break it into smaller steps. This closes the loop and sets up the next day.

## Action Steps Summary
1. **Dump and Rank** — Brain dump all tasks, pick three big rocks, and label A/B/C so the list serves you instead of scaring you.  
2. **Block and Guard** — Schedule A-tasks in prime hours, batch the rest, and remove distractions so important work actually gets time.  
3. **Finish and Reset** — Complete A-work in focus sprints, check off wins, and rebuild tomorrow’s list so progress compounds all week.

## FAQ
**Why do I stay busy all day and still miss the work that counts?**  
Busy work is often urgent noise. Without a ranked top three, the loud tasks win and the high-impact tasks wait.

**Why does my to-do list grow even when I work hard?**  
You are capturing tasks but not sorting them. A list without A/B/C order becomes a pile, not a plan.

**Why do I keep moving the same task to tomorrow?**  
It is either too big, not truly important, or never given a protected time block. Split it, cut it, or schedule it first.

**Why can’t I focus for more than a few minutes?**  
Your A-work is competing with open alerts and mixed tasks. Silence inputs and run one priority in a short sprint.

**Why do evenings still fill with leftover work?**  
Low-value tasks ate the morning. Put big rocks first, then fit B and C work around them—not the other way around.
