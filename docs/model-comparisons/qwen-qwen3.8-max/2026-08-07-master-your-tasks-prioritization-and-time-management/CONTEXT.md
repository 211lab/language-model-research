## Title
Finish What Matters First

## Subtitle
Stop letting loud, small tasks push out the work that moves you forward.

## Promise
Complete your three most important tasks before the end of the workday for five days in a row without working late, skipping breaks, or answering every request as it arrives.

## Motivations
- Finish key work while you still have energy.
- Start each day with a clear first task.
- Move real projects forward each week.
- End work with a checked list, not a growing one.
- Keep time for home duties after work.

## Challenges & Sacrifices
- A boss adds urgent requests after you set your plan.
- Family needs interrupt your best work hours.
- Chat, email, and phone alerts break your focus.
- Small chores feel quick and push big tasks out.
- Saying no or later can delay help for someone else.

## Frustrations
- You work all day but cannot name one big result.
- You lie awake thinking about tasks you forgot.
- You open many tasks and close few.
- You hate small requests because they steal your hours.
- You stop trusting your daily plan because it keeps breaking.

## Transformations
- From busy motion to finished top tasks.
- From night worry to a written plan for tomorrow.
- From scattered starts to closed work blocks.
- From anger at requests to a clear no or later slot.
- From broken plans to a daily order you can follow.

## Choice
You can keep letting the loudest task run your day. That path leaves important work late, half-done, or pushed to tomorrow again. Or you can pick your top three, give them real time, and finish what matters first. That path gives you a done list and a stronger end to the day.

## Actionable VERB
FILTER

## Model
**Model name:** FILTER

A beginner starts with tasks stuck in their head. An expert ends with a written, prioritized plan. FILTER moves you from noise to done.

### F — Fetch every task
**Context:** You feel overloaded because tasks are scattered across your head, notes, email, and messages.  
**Do this:** Write every task into one list. Include work projects, calls, errands, and home duties. Do not sort them yet.

### I — Isolate your top three
**Context:** Every task starts to look equally urgent.  
**Do this:** Ask, “If I only finish three things today, which three will create the biggest impact?” Mark those as A tasks. Label the next group B and the small tasks C.

### L — Limit urgent noise
**Context:** Urgent but minor tasks keep taking time from important work.  
**Do this:** Sort tasks by urgent and important. Delegate, delay, batch, or remove what does not belong in today’s top work. Say no or later to tasks that only feel urgent.

### T — Time-block the big rocks
**Context:** Important tasks disappear when they have no protected time.  
**Do this:** Put your A tasks into your calendar during your best energy period. Use 25-minute work sprints with short breaks. Treat these blocks like real appointments.

### E — Execute in order
**Context:** Multitasking makes you feel busy but slows real progress.  
**Do this:** Silence alerts, close extra tabs, and work on A1 first. Then move to A2 and A3. Batch similar B and C tasks into one lower-energy block.

### R — Review and reset
**Context:** Without a review, the same unfinished tasks keep returning.  
**Do this:** Check off what you finished. Move unfinished tasks to the next day, cut what no longer matters, and break stuck tasks into smaller steps. Write tomorrow’s top three before you stop work. This completes the lesson.

## Action Steps Summary
1. **Capture and Choose**  
   Empty your mind into one list and pick three big rocks. You start with a clear target.

2. **Block and Begin**  
   Put the big rocks on your calendar and work them first. Your best hours go to your best tasks.

3. **Review and Reset**  
   Check off results, move leftovers, and set tomorrow’s top three. Each day ends with proof and a plan.

## FAQ
**Why am I busy all day but still behind?**  
Urgent noise is setting your order. Pick three important tasks before you open requests.

**Why do I keep doing small chores first?**  
They are easy wins. Sort by impact and start with the first big rock.

**Why do interruptions ruin my plan?**  
Your top tasks have no protected time. Give them a time block and silence alerts.

**Why do I avoid big projects?**  
The task is too vague. Write the next visible step and work for 25 minutes.

**Why is saying no so hard?**  
You see it as a personal refusal. Treat it as choosing one task over another.
