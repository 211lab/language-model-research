## Model
**P - Pick Your Big Rocks**
Start every morning by identifying the 2-3 most critical tasks that will have the biggest impact. Do not start with your inbox. Write them down first. These are your "Big Rocks." If you do nothing else today, these must be done. This step forces you to define success before you start working.

**R - Remove the Noise**
Once your priorities are set, eliminate distractions. Silence your phone, close unrelated browser tabs, and inform others you are in focus mode. Create a barrier between you and the interruptions. This step protects your energy for the high-value work you identified in the first step.

**I - Implement in Blocks**
Schedule specific time blocks for your Big Rocks. Use the Pomodoro technique (25 minutes work, 5 minutes break) to maintain intensity. Treat these blocks like unmissable appointments with yourself. Do not switch tasks. Finish one block before starting the next. This step ensures execution through focused effort.

**O - Organize the Rest**
After completing your Big Rocks, handle the remaining tasks. Group similar small tasks together (batching), such as answering emails or making calls. Schedule these for lower-energy times in the day. This step prevents trivial tasks from consuming your prime energy.

**R - Review and Reset**
At the end of the day, review what you accomplished. Check off completed tasks. Carry over unfinished items to tomorrow’s list, reprioritizing as needed. Ask yourself: "Did I do the right things?" This step closes the loop and prepares you for the next day.

**I - Iterate Daily**
Consistency is key. Repeat this process every single day. Adjust your priorities based on new information, but never skip the process. This step builds the habit of strategic thinking over time.

**T - Track Your Progress**
Keep a simple log of your daily priorities and outcomes. Look for patterns. Are you consistently failing to complete Big Rocks? Why? Adjust your time estimates or environment. This step provides data to improve your system.

**O - Optimize Your Environment**
Continuously improve your workspace and tools. Remove physical clutter. Use digital tools to capture tasks so nothing falls through the cracks. A clean environment supports a clear mind. This step reduces friction in your workflow.

**Z - Zero Out the Day**
End your day with a clear mind. Ensure your to-do list for tomorrow is ready. Do not leave loose ends that cause anxiety overnight. This step allows for true rest and mental recovery.

## Action Steps Summary
1. **Define the Big Rocks:** Identify your top 3 critical tasks each morning before checking email or social media.
2. **Execute in Deep Focus:** Work on these tasks first using time blocks and distraction-free environments.
3. **Review and Reset:** End each day by reviewing outcomes and preparing the next day’s priorities to ensure continuity.

## FAQ
**Why do I feel busy but not productive?**
You are likely reacting to urgent but unimportant tasks. Prioritization forces you to focus on important tasks that drive real results, not just noise.

**What if an emergency comes up?**
Prioritization is dynamic. If a true emergency arises, pause, reassess your Big Rocks, and adjust. But do not let minor interruptions derail your main focus.

**How do I say no to people?**
Use your prioritized list as your guide. If a request does not align with your top 3 tasks, it is not a priority. Politely decline or delegate it.

**What if I can’t finish my Big Rocks?**
Break them into smaller steps. If a task is too big, it is not actionable. Make it smaller so you can complete it and build momentum.

**Does this work for creative work?**
Yes. Creative work requires deep focus. By blocking time and removing distractions, you create the mental space needed for high-quality creative output.
