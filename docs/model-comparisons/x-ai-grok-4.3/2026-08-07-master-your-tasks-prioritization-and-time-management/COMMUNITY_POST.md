## Post

A single sheet sits at the edge of the desk each morning. Lines run down it in uneven handwriting. By evening the paper has barely moved, and most of the marks remain exactly where they started.

The day fills with other things. Messages land, meetings appear, small requests multiply. Each one presses, yet nothing gathers enough force to finish what actually matters. The list grows because there is no daily cutoff enforced by action.

This week we are looking at how to name the three tasks that would still matter if nothing else moved forward, then protect the first hours to finish them before the rest of the day takes over. The lesson and lecture exist to make that choice workable in practice.

The worksheet is the tool you will use to run the pattern for four days. Write every task down, circle the three that carry the load, block time before 2 p.m., and move only the unfinished top items to the next list. Do the worksheet first. Bring what you notice when the group meets.
