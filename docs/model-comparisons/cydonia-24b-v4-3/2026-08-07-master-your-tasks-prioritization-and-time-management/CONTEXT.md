## Actionable VERB
**C L E A R**

## Model

### C - Capture
Start each morning (or the night before) with a brain dump. Write down every single task, idea, and commitment floating in your head—work projects, personal errands, emails to send, calls to make, everything. Don't judge or organize yet. Just get it all out of your head and onto paper or a digital list. This clears mental clutter and ensures nothing falls through the cracks. Set a timer for 5 minutes and write without stopping.

### L - Label
Now review your complete list and label each task with one letter:
- **A** = Top 3 priorities that will make the biggest impact today
- **B** = Important but not urgent, or second-tier tasks
- **C** = Minor errands and maintenance items
- **D** = Can wait until tomorrow or later this week
- **E** = Can be delegated or eliminated entirely

Be honest. If a task doesn't serve your goals or critical duties, label it D or E. This is where you practice saying "no" to yourself.

### E - Execute
Schedule your A-tasks first. Block time in your calendar for each one, especially during your peak energy hours (usually morning). During these blocks, practice deep focus: silence your phone, close unnecessary tabs, and work on one A-task until it's complete or your time block ends. Use the Pomodoro technique if it helps: 25 minutes of focused work, then a 5-minute break. Repeat. Treat these blocks like unmissable appointments with yourself.

### A - Adjust
At the end of each day, review what you accomplished. Check off completed tasks (the physical act of checking gives your brain a satisfaction boost). For unfinished A-tasks, move them to tomorrow's list and ask: "Is this still a priority, or do I need to break it into smaller steps?" If something consistently isn't getting done, it might not be as important as you thought—reconsider its label. Adjust tomorrow's plan based on what you learned today.

### R - Release
This is the hardest but most important step. Release attachment to getting everything done. You cannot do it all, and that's not a failure—it's reality. Release guilt about unfinished C-tasks. Release the need to respond immediately to every notification. Release the idea that busy equals productive. By releasing these mental burdens, you free up mental energy to focus on what truly matters. Your peace of mind is more valuable than a completely empty to-do list.

## Action Steps Summary

**1. Morning Capture & Label**
Spend 10 minutes each morning dumping all tasks and labeling them A, B, C, D, or E. This creates clarity and ensures nothing important gets missed.

**2. Priority Execution Blocks**
Schedule your top 3 A-tasks during your best energy hours and work them with deep focus, treating these blocks as non-negotiable appointments.

**3. Daily Adjust & Release**
Review your progress at day's end, adjust tomorrow's plan based on what you learned, and consciously release guilt about unfinished low-priority tasks.

## FAQ

**Q: What if I have more than 3 truly important tasks each day?**
A: If you consistently have more than 3 A-tasks, you're either overcommitting or not being honest about what's truly critical. Challenge yourself: if you could only accomplish 3 things today, which ones would create the most positive impact? Those are your real A-tasks. The others are probably B or C tasks.

**Q: What if something urgent comes up that wasn't on my list?**
A: First, ask: "Is this truly urgent, or just feels urgent because someone else wants it now?" Real emergencies are rare. If it's genuinely urgent, handle it quickly, then return to your priority work. If it's just someone else's priority, schedule it for later or delegate it.

**Q: Should I prioritize based on what takes the least time?**
A: No. Prioritize based on impact, not ease. Easy tasks give quick dopamine hits but don't move you forward. Hard, important tasks create real progress. Your system should force you to do the hard important work first, not the easy unimportant work.

**Q: What if I don't finish my A-tasks even with this system?**
A: This means you're either overestimating how much you can accomplish in a day or your A-tasks are too large. Break big tasks into smaller steps (like "write email draft" instead of "complete project"). Also, be honest about your energy and time constraints. It's better to complete 2 A-tasks than to spread yourself thin across 5.

**Q: Do I need special apps or tools for this to work?**
A: No. A simple notebook works just as well as expensive apps. The system works because of your discipline and decision-making, not because of the tool. Start with paper if that removes friction. The method matters more than the medium.
