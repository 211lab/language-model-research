## Title
Do Your Top 3 Tasks First and Take Back Your Day

## Subtitle
Stop letting small chores run your life. Learn to sort, schedule, and protect the work that truly matters.

## Promise
You will check off your 3 most important tasks by noon each workday, tracked by your planner marks, without adding overtime, skipping meals, or cutting exercise.

## Motivations
1. Finish key projects on time.
2. Feel in control of the day.
3. Have free time after the big work is done.
4. See the task list get shorter.
5. Show family and bosses they can count on you.

## Challenges & Sacrifices
1. Email alerts break your focus every few minutes.
2. A boss drops a small urgent request mid-morning.
3. Kids need help right when you planned to work.
4. Phone pings pull you into social apps.
5. You try to do every task and end up worn out.

## Frustrations
- Watching the day end with big tasks still not done.
- Feeling busy all day but not moving forward.
- Knowing you did small stuff while key work slipped.
- Regretting saying yes to low-value favors.
- Lying awake worried about tomorrow's pile.

## Transformations
- From big tasks left undone to seeing them checked before lunch.
- From busy but stuck to making real progress each day.
- From reacting to noise to working on what counts.
- From guilt after saying yes to calm after saying no.
- From night-time worry to a clear plan for tomorrow.

## Choice
You can keep living the hard way: end each day tired, with key work ignored and stress high. Or you can choose to act now, learn to TRIAGE your tasks, and start walking toward the calm control you want most—finished big rocks and a free afternoon.

## Actionable VERB
TRIAGE

## Model
T – Task Dump: At day start, write every task in your head on paper or app. Get it all out so nothing hides. This clears your mind and shows the full load.

R – Rank: Mark the top 3 that make the biggest impact as A. Label next as B, small errands as C. Note what can wait or be given to someone else. This sorts the list by value.

I – Identify Big Rocks: From the A marks, pick the 2–3 that align with your goals. These are your big rocks for the day. They get first claim on your energy.

A – Allocate: Block early time in your schedule for those big rocks. Use a 25-minute focus sprint if needed. Treat that block like a meeting you cannot miss.

G – Guard: When the block starts, silence phone, close tabs, tell others you are focused. Work only on that task until done or time up. Protecting focus gets it finished.

E – Evaluate: At day end, check off done items, move leftovers to tomorrow, and ask if a task is truly needed. This closes the loop and sharpens tomorrow’s list. With this step, you complete the lesson and own your task list.

## Action Steps Summary
1. Dump and Rank: Write all tasks and label the top three A-level items to gain a clear priority list.
2. Block and Guard: Schedule big rocks early and shield that time from distractions to ensure completion.
3. Check and Reset: Review checks each evening and carry forward only what still matters to start fresh.

## FAQ
- Why do I always feel behind?
You are likely doing urgent small tasks first. Use TRIAGE to pick the few that matter most and do them early.

- Why does my to-do list never shrink?
Because you add without sorting. Dump and rank daily so low-value items get dropped or delayed.

- How come I’m tired but nothing big is done?
You let distractions break your focus. Guard a time block for big rocks and watch them finish.

- How do I say no without feeling bad?
See “no” as protecting your A tasks. Delegating or delaying C tasks is part of smart prioritization.

- What should I do with many small errands?
Batch them in low-energy times after big rocks are done, so they don’t steal your best hours.
