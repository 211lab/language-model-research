## Post

The coffee mug sat cold on the bench at seven. By evening it was still full. The big job never started. The day filled with pings, small asks, easy errands. That quiet failure repeats for most of us.

We keep living that pattern until something breaks the loop. This week we get concrete about sorting what matters from what merely makes noise. The lecture and lesson will show the method to dump every task, rank the few that carry real weight, and protect the early hours for that work.

But the real shift happens on paper. The worksheet coming your way is the tool the group will work from together. It turns the idea into a daily practice you can mark, guard, and review. Before we meet to talk it through, you need that sheet in your hands. The group conversation will go deep only if you've already picked your top three and felt the pull of distractions against them.

Think about the usual traps: email alerts every few minutes, a boss's mid-morning request, kids needing help right as you sit down. The worksheet is built to absorb that heat. It gives you a place to put the pressure so the work that counts actually catches. If you show up to the discussion without it, you'll be listening to others describe a fire you haven't lit.

So here's the move: when the worksheet lands, don't file it. Dump your tasks on it first thing tomorrow. Rank the three that change your day. Block the time. Then hold that experience until we gather. The lecture will make sense of what you hit. The group will sharpen what you struggled with. The worksheet is where the week turns from busy to owned.

We'll talk soon about what it felt like to guard that block. Bring the marks, not just opinions.
