The day starts with a full head and a quiet plan. Then the phone pings, a boss drops a small urgent request, kids need help right when you planned to work, and email alerts break your focus every few minutes. By evening you are tired, but the big tasks sit untouched. You did small stuff while key work slipped. You feel busy all day but not moving forward. This is not because you are broken; it is because you are undisciplined in how you sort pressure. The truth is simple: you must do your top three tasks first—not the easy ones, not the loud ones, but the few that make the biggest impact. If you check those off by noon, the day is yours, and the rest can wait or be given away. The TRIAGE method is the process that takes back control. As you complete this worksheet, you will move through each letter of TRIAGE to build a daily system that finishes your three most important tasks by noon without adding overtime, skipping meals, or cutting exercise.

## T – Task Dump

At the start of the Task Dump section, write every task in your head on the worksheet lines. Get it all out so nothing hides. This clears your mind and shows the full load you are carrying.

- **Avoid:**
  - Writing only work tasks and leaving out personal errands, family needs, or nagging reminders.
  - Using vague entries like “stuff” or “things to do” that hide the real load.
  - Filtering or ranking while dumping; that defeats the purge.
- **Works better:**
  - Recording concrete items: “Submit quarterly report,” “Call plumber about leak,” “Read 10 pages of training manual.”
  - Including every ping: “Reply to boss’s mid-morning request,” “Help kids with homework at 4pm.”
  - Capturing the full spread from big projects to small chores so the page reflects reality.

## R – Rank

On the Rank lines, mark the top 3 that make the biggest impact as A. Label the next as B, and small errands as C. Note what can wait or be given to someone else. This sorts the list by value.

- **Avoid:**
  - Marking more than three as A; diluting the top tier loses focus.
  - Labeling tasks by ease or loudness rather than impact.
  - Skipping the note of what can wait or be delegated; that leaves clutter.
- **Works better:**
  - Choosing A items that align with key goals: “Finish client proposal” not “Check email.”
  - Assigning B to important but secondary: “Draft outline for next week’s meeting.”
  - Assigning C to errands: “Buy groceries,” “Print documents.” Writing “Delegate to spouse: school pickup” or “Postpone: organize closet.”

## I – Identify Big Rocks

From the A marks, pick the 2–3 that align with your goals. Write them as your Big Rocks for the day in the Identify section. They get first claim on your energy.

- **Avoid:**
  - Picking A tasks that are urgent but not goal-aligned; if an A doesn’t match goals, reconsider ranking.
  - Listing more than three big rocks; the model specifies 2–3.
  - Vague goal references; tie to specific outcome.
- **Works better:**
  - Selecting “Complete grant application (goal: fund project)” as big rock.
  - Writing “Exercise 30 min (goal: health)” if it is A and goal-aligned.
  - Stating clearly: “Big Rock 1: Finish presentation deck; Big Rock 2: Review contract; Big Rock 3: Plan weekly meals.”

## A – Allocate

In the Allocate block, block early time in your schedule for those big rocks. Use a 25-minute focus sprint if needed. Treat that block like a meeting you cannot miss. Write the specific time slots on the worksheet.

- **Avoid:**
  - Placing big rocks in leftover evening hours when energy is low.
  - Vague “sometime today” entries; they invite displacement.
  - Blocking time without committing to a start action.
- **Works better:**
  - Writing “8:00–8:25 AM: Big Rock 1 focus sprint” and “8:30–9:00 AM: Big Rock 2.”
  - Using early morning or first work hour: “7:30 AM before email.”
  - Noting “Treat as unmissable meeting: no exceptions.”

## G – Guard

On the Guard lines, specify how you will silence phone, close tabs, tell others you are focused, and work only on that task until done or time up. Protecting focus gets it finished.

- **Avoid:**
  - Generic “avoid distractions” without concrete actions.
  - Assuming others know you’re busy; write the exact message you’ll give.
  - Planning to multitask during the block.
- **Works better:**
  - Writing “Phone on airplane mode, Slack closed, tell coworker: ‘Heads-down until 9’.”
  - Stating “Work only on Big Rock 1 until 8:25, then stop or finish.”
  - Listing physical barriers: “Go to quiet room, close browser except needed doc.”

## E – Evaluate

At the Evaluate section, record done items checked off, leftovers moved to tomorrow, and ask if a task is truly needed. This closes the loop and sharpens tomorrow’s list. With this step you complete the lesson and own your task list.

- **Avoid:**
  - Skipping the review because you’re tired; that loses the gain.
  - Carrying over everything unchanged; re-rank.
  - Ignoring tasks that never get done without questioning necessity.
- **Works better:**
  - Checking off “Big Rock 1 done,” “Big Rock 2 done,” moving “Big Rock 3” to tomorrow A.
  - Writing “Task ‘organize desk’ carried 3 days—cut it or delegate?”
  - Noting “Boss request handled, not A, delegated kid pickup to partner.”

Finish the worksheet now. Write the dump, mark the A’s, set the block, guard it, and evaluate tonight. Act on what you wrote tomorrow at dawn: take the paper, do your top three first, and own the day. No one will do it for you; the method is on the page and the choice is yours.
