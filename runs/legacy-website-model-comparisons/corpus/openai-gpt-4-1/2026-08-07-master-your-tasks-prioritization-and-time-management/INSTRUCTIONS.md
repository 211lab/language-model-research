# Master Your Tasks: Prioritization and Time Management

Effective task management isn’t about doing more—it’s about doing what matters most. If you’re ending your days feeling busy but not accomplished, you’re likely letting urgent distractions crowd out your real priorities. The truth is, you don’t have a task problem; you have a prioritization problem. The FILTER model is your daily system for cutting through the noise, focusing your energy, and finishing each day with real wins. Use this worksheet to apply the FILTER process step by step, so you can take control of your to-do list and make steady progress on what truly counts.

---

## F – Focus First

Begin by emptying your mind of every task, responsibility, or errand you’re carrying. Don’t filter or judge—just list everything you think you need to do today. Once your list is complete, ask yourself: “If I only get three things done today, which will make the biggest difference?” Mark these as your top priorities.

**What to avoid:**
- Listing only the obvious or easy tasks (“check email,” “water plants”).
- Skipping the brain dump and trying to prioritize from memory.
- Picking tasks based on urgency alone, not impact.

**What works better:**
- Writing down every task, big or small, work or personal.
- Including tasks you’ve been putting off or that feel uncomfortable.
- Choosing your top three based on what will move you forward in your most important roles or goals.

---

## I – Identify Importance

Now, sort your list using a simple system like the Eisenhower Matrix: urgent vs. important. Move the most important tasks—those that align with your goals or have lasting impact—to the top. Make sure your top three are truly important, not just loud or time-sensitive.

**What to avoid:**
- Confusing urgency with importance (e.g., “reply to every email”).
- Letting other people’s demands automatically take priority.
- Keeping your list in a random order.

**What works better:**
- Asking, “Will this matter a week from now? A month from now?”
- Marking tasks that are both important and urgent, but prioritizing those that are important even if not urgent.
- Reordering your list so the most meaningful tasks are at the top.

---

## L – Lock Time

Block out specific times on your calendar for your top three tasks. Treat these blocks as non-negotiable appointments. Schedule them when your energy is highest—often first thing in the morning.

**What to avoid:**
- Leaving your top tasks for “when you have time.”
- Scheduling important work during low-energy periods or when you’re likely to be interrupted.
- Allowing meetings or minor chores to fill your best hours.

**What works better:**
- Physically writing or typing your top three tasks into your calendar.
- Protecting these blocks from other commitments.
- Matching your most demanding tasks to your peak focus times.

---

## T – Tune Out Distractions

When it’s time to work on a priority, defend your focus. Silence your phone, close unrelated tabs, and let others know you’re in focus mode. Use a timer (like the Pomodoro technique: 25 minutes work, 5 minutes break) to stay on track.

**What to avoid:**
- Keeping notifications on or checking your phone “just in case.”
- Multitasking or leaving email open in the background.
- Allowing interruptions from colleagues, family, or social media.

**What works better:**
- Setting your phone to Do Not Disturb and putting it out of reach.
- Closing all apps and tabs not needed for the task.
- Communicating your focus time to others and using a visible signal if needed.

---

## E – Execute Relentlessly

Work on your top tasks until they’re done or your scheduled time block is up. Don’t let less important chores or sudden requests steal your attention. Push through resistance and finish what you started.

**What to avoid:**
- Switching to easier or less important tasks when you hit a challenge.
- Letting minor interruptions derail your progress.
- Stopping before your time block is finished without a good reason.

**What works better:**
- Committing to stay with the task for the full block, even if it’s uncomfortable.
- Noting distractions on a separate sheet to handle later.
- Celebrating completion of each priority task to build momentum.

---

## R – Review and Reprioritize

At the end of the day, check off what you finished. Move any unfinished tasks to tomorrow’s list, but first ask if they’re still important. Adjust your plan as needed to keep moving forward—cut or delegate what no longer matters.

**What to avoid:**
- Ignoring unfinished tasks and letting them pile up.
- Automatically carrying everything forward without questioning its value.
- Skipping the review and missing the chance to improve.

**What works better:**
- Physically checking off completed tasks for a sense of accomplishment.
- Reviewing why something didn’t get done—was it not important, too big, or blocked?
- Pruning your list so only what truly matters stays on tomorrow’s plan.

---

Finish your worksheet with honesty and specificity. The FILTER process only works if you’re ruthless about what matters and disciplined about defending your priorities. Use what you’ve written to guide your actions tomorrow—don’t let your day get hijacked. The more consistently you FILTER, the more control and progress you’ll see.
