## Subject

Master Your Tasks: Put the Important Work First

## Body

You can work all day and still avoid the work that matters.

This week, you will build a short, ranked plan that protects your most important work before messages, minor requests, and easy tasks take over. The goal is not to finish everything. The goal is to complete the work that earns its place.

Your packet includes the lesson as an attached PDF, the **Master Your Tasks** worksheet, and a link to this week’s public letter. Use the worksheet as your central execution tool for the next seven days. Complete it before lower-priority work begins, choose exactly three priorities, and aim to complete at least two before lesser tasks.

The lesson explains why busy days so often produce little meaningful progress. Small tasks offer quick relief and visible completion, while important work requires sustained attention and a deliberate decision. When every task has equal access to your attention, the loudest request controls your day.

The public letter develops that idea through the image of a cold coffee mug left beside unfinished work. Your day can become crowded with activity while the report, proposal, project, or conversation that matters keeps moving to tomorrow. The answer is not perfect control over every minute. It is an order strong enough to help you decide what deserves your attention when interruptions arrive.

The letter also shows how repeated choices become evidence. Each time you protect important work, delay a low-value request, or remove a task that no longer matters, you strengthen your ability to choose. A short list becomes more trustworthy because you use it, review it, and adjust it honestly.

Work through the worksheet in order using FILTER:

- **Find every task:** Capture work, errands, calls, messages, duties, appointments, and commitments in one visible list.
- **Identify what matters:** Mark what is urgent, important, both, or neither, then choose your three big rocks.
- **Limit today’s list:** Delete, delay, delegate, schedule, decline, or hold everything that does not belong today.
- **Time-block the priorities:** Give each big rock a start time, stopping point, first action, and concrete result.
- **Execute in order:** Begin with the first priority before minor messages or easy chores.
- **Review and reset:** Record what happened, decide what unfinished work still matters, and set tomorrow’s first block.

Do not use the worksheet to describe the organized person you hope to become. Use it to make visible decisions: what you will complete, what you will delay, who will handle what you delegate, and what you will remove. The completed worksheet should produce a realistic daily plan and a week of evidence about where your attention actually goes.

Read this week’s newsletter, **“Get Your Three Most Important Tasks Done First,”** to understand why prioritization matters now. It will help you connect daily task choices to the larger process of building a reliable personal system: gather the pressure, direct it toward meaningful work, and repeat the actions that turn intention into useful results.

Use the GPT coach alongside the worksheet when you get stuck. It will guide you through FILTER one step at a time, challenge vague tasks, help you rank competing demands, build workable time blocks, and review the evidence without letting you hide behind general intentions. Bring it concrete information and let it push you toward decisions you can act on.

Your task list does not have to control you. Capture it, rank it, protect the first priority, and prove the order through action. For the next seven days, choose what leads—and let your completed work show that you can follow through.
